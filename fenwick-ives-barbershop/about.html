The Ledger

How we got from Haywood Road to Cedar Row

1987

Two chairs, one barber, one pole

Walt Fenwick opens a two-chair shop on Haywood Road with a barber pole he found at a salvage yard and rewired himself. He cuts every head of hair for the first seven years.

1994

First hire

Business is steady enough to add a second chair and hire Sam Ochoa, a barber Walt met at a trade show in Charlotte. Sam stays fourteen years.

2009

Denise takes over

Walt's daughter Denise — who'd been sweeping floors and answering phones there since she was seven — takes the shop over and renames it Fenwick & Ives, after her late business partner and mentor Carl Ives.

2016

The move

The Haywood Road building sells to a condo developer. Denise finds a storefront four blocks over on Cedar Row and the whole shop — pole, chairs, and the original 1987 ledger book — moves in one weekend.

2021

Four chairs, record wall, reopened

A remodel adds a third and fourth chair, a wall of Denise's father's record collection, and gets the original pole restored a second time by a specialist in Knoxville.

2026

Third generation

Denise's son Theo is now training the shop's first outside apprentice who isn't family — a sign, Denise says, that the shop's finally big enough to need one.

Behind The Chairs

The people cutting your hair

DF

Denise Fenwick-Ives

Owner & Master Barber — 30 years

Walt's daughter, and the reason the shop is still here. Specializes in classic scissor cuts and straight razor shaves. Keeps a standing Thursday 6pm slot booked eleven years running.

TF

Theo Fenwick

Barber — 6 years

Denise's son and the shop's third generation. Handles most of the fades and all of the kids' cuts — he's the one who runs the fire-truck chair.

RB

Reggie Boudreaux

Barber — 12 years

Came up from New Orleans in 2014. Sharpens every straight razor in the shop by hand every Sunday night and won't let anyone else touch his strop.

PA

Priya Anand

Barber — 4 years

Trained in Chicago before moving to Asheville in 2022. The shop's go-to for curly and textured cuts, and the only one who can talk Denise into trying new clipper guards.

House Rules

How we actually run the shop

No TV News

The shop TV plays whatever game is on, or nothing. No cable news, ever — that rule predates the shop's second chair.

Biscuit

The shop dog works Wednesdays and half of Saturday. He sheds on the black chairs specifically, out of everyone's, spite.

The Ledger

Walt's original 1987 appointment book sits behind the register. Everyone who's ever sat in that chair is still written in it somewhere.

Local Only

We stock beard oil and pomade from two other Asheville small businesses. Nothing shipped in from a warehouse if we can help it.

Around Town

A little recognition

"Best Barbershop, West Asheville" — two years running, and Denise still says it doesn't count until they win it a third time.
